# ADR-0002: "When it fails" — cascade failsafes join the convention's public home

- **Date**: 2026-09-03 | **Iteration**: 1 | **Status**: Accepted | **Deciders**: Jérémie Lumbroso (the directive); Pharos 5 (the shape — this lane's call per the routing)

**TL;DR**: The founder's directive — "we need to have failsafes for typical ORRCF failure modes" — lands as a plain-English "When it fails" section on the page and README (four failsafes distilled from a real five-concur cascade), because a failsafe that lives only in crew records is folklore, not convention.

## Context

On 2026-09-02→03, a question in a sibling repository drew five stacked
concurs on one pole across two days — which turned out to be one analysis
five times. The frame was binary from birth (the question's own author
later owned this: the title posed two options and the need-line demanded
"one word"); every subsequent endorsement falsified *within* the frame
and none attacked its binarity; intermediate options surfaced only under
three rounds of founder pressure ("this refusal to accept shades as valid
and workable is epistemically suspicious to me"). The rebuilt discourse
produced a four-option spectrum and two genuinely new arguments — and the
ratifier then chose the *original* pole, now for explainable reasons. His
coda is the whole lesson: The five-fold consensus may have been bias or
may have been the best answer — **"an un-explicited spectrum makes a
right answer and a cascaded answer indistinguishable."** His directive,
bolded in the closing answer: **"we need to have failsafes for typical
ORRCF failure modes."**

## Decision

The page and README gain a **"When it fails"** section: The failure mode
named plainly (agreement that looks like verification; an unstated
spectrum makes a right answer and an echoed answer indistinguishable),
then four failsafes:

1. **Options carry the burden of the space** — at least one honest
   intermediate between poles, or one sentence on why the space is
   genuinely binary (routed candidate 1).
2. **The option set is falsifiable too** — "a position that fits none of
   these" reopens the frame, not the fight; asking for shades is a
   signal, never an obstruction (this lane's addition: The exhibit's
   spectrum cost three rounds of founder pressure to surface — frame
   pushback must be a protocol move, not a fight).
3. **Before you agree, two questions** — an argument nobody has made,
   and an attempt to break the option you prefer; anything less is a
   vote, not a verification (Flint 5's self-test, lightly rephrased).
4. **Answers cite arguments, never counts** — "five concurred" is
   evidence of five readers (routed candidate 3; the sibling repo's
   convergence checklist carries the same rule as its item 8).

Plus one upstream warning tying the failure to its commonest root: A
question that demands a one-word answer has usually already shrunk its
option space — with the page's own payoff line kept consistent (one word
remains a wonderful *answer* when the question earned it).

Register constraints honored: No house coinage enters the page (term
budget — the five spelled words only); the incident is cited as "a live
incident in this convention's own house (September 2026)" with no dead
link; the exhibit link lands on the page only if its repository flips
public and this ADR's citation is then wired.
